A Friendly Guide to Carrom: Board, Rules and Shots

Welcome to Playstreet, a simple information page about the kind of play people enjoy every day. Today’s topic is carrom, a flick-and-pocket board game played in homes, clubs and tournaments across many countries.

This guide explains the board, the pieces, the rules, the main shots and some care tips, one topic at a time, with pictures.

The basics

What Is Carrom?

Carrom is a tabletop game that grew popular in South Asia and is now played worldwide. Two or four players sit around a square board and use a small disc, called the striker, to flick lightweight coins into four corner pockets. Play rewards a steady hand, patience and a little planning, and a full round usually fits into half an hour.

It needs very little space, no electricity and almost no setup, which is why families have enjoyed it for generations.

Equipment

Board and Equipment

Here are the main parts of a carrom set, so you know exactly what each one does.

  • Board: a smooth, flat wooden square with raised wooden sides, called cushions, that let coins bounce back.
  • Pockets: four holes in the corners, each with a small net underneath.
  • Baselines: marked lines on each side. The striker is placed on your own baseline between the two end circles.
  • Centre circle: where the coins are arranged at the start.
  • Coins: nine white, nine black and one red coin, the queen. Each is about 3 cm wide.
  • Striker: a slightly larger disc, about 4 cm wide, that you flick with a finger.
  • Carrom powder: a fine, dry powder that helps the pieces glide smoothly.
Rules

How to Play: The Rules

The rules are short. Here is the common way to play, though local house rules can differ.

  1. Set up: place the queen in the centre, ring it with alternating white and black coins, then add an outer ring, as shown in the picture.
  2. Choose sides: players toss to decide who goes first. The first player usually takes white.
  3. Strike: place the striker on your baseline and flick it so it hits a coin. Only the striker may be touched.
  4. Pocket: if you pocket one of your own colour, you play again.
  5. Queen: pocketing the queen counts only if you then pocket one of your own coins in the same turn, which is called “covering” it.
  6. Fouls: if the striker falls into a pocket, one of your pocketed coins goes back to the centre.
  7. Score: when one player clears all their coins, they earn one point for each opposing coin left on the board, plus three if they hold the queen. Many rounds are played to 25 points.
Technique

Four Basic Shots

A few basic shots cover most situations. Practise each one slowly before trying to hit hard.

Direct shot

Hit the coin straight on so it travels toward the pocket in a line. Start here.

Rebound shot

Send the coin off a cushion first. Useful when the direct path to the pocket is blocked.

Cut shot

Strike the coin on its edge so it moves at an angle, away from the striker’s path.

Break shot

The opening shot that spreads the coins. Aim for the centre with steady, firm force.

Ways to play

Popular Ways to Play

Singles

Two players, one colour each. It is the best way to learn aim and patience.

Doubles

Two teams of two, with partners sitting opposite each other. Turns pass clockwise, and teamwork matters.

Freestyle

Any coin pocketed scores for the player who pockets it. Good for a relaxed, quick round with beginners.

Why people enjoy it

Focus

Each shot asks you to think about angle, force and the next turn.

Coordination

Flicking the striker trains steady finger control and a good eye.

Together time

It is easy to learn, so different ages can play at the same table.

Calm pace

There are no screens and no rush, so it is a relaxing break.

Wrap-up

Final Thoughts

Carrom is easy to start and takes years to master. Learn the board, keep the rules in mind, practise the four basic shots and look after your equipment, and every round becomes smoother. Playstreet hopes this guide helps you understand carrom clearly and enjoy it with friends and family.

Care tips

Keep the board flat and dry, wipe it with a soft dry cloth, sprinkle a little fresh powder before play, and store the coins in a small box so none are lost.

Common questions

Do I need a big table?

No. A standard board is about 80 cm across with its frame, and a sturdy small table or stand is enough.

What if I pocket the striker?

That is a foul in most rules. You usually return one of your previously pocketed coins to the centre, and your turn ends.

Is carrom hard to learn?

The rules take a few minutes. Accurate aim takes practice, but beginners enjoy playing from the first turn.
